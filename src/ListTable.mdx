# ListTable

A list of things on a page, under column headings: each row leads to its thing, a few short facts in columns, and an action at its end.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.Default} />

## When

- **A page that lists things to find and open**: every topic, every team's
  places, every project — each row its name and what it is about, then its
  facts (its team, who is in it, when it was last active), then what you can
  do from the list (Join).
- `columns`: the facts after the name, each with its heading and its width —
  `small` 80px (a number, a few faces), `medium` 96px (a date), `large`
  112px (a name).
- `keep`: which columns give way when the list runs short of room — `low`
  ones first, then `high` ones. The name and the end never do. The list
  measures **its own** width, not the window's: beside a sidebar it gives way
  as it would in a small window.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.Narrow} />

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.Narrowest} />

- No `columns` (`[]`): a list with no facts to show — the rows alone, with
  no headings; the heading over the part names the list.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.NoColumns} />

- `headings={false}`: its facts with no headings on screen, where the
  heading over the part names the list and the facts read without a label
  (faces and a number, "Yesterday"). Screen readers still read each cell's
  heading.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.NoHeadings} />

- `end`: the rows hold their own control at the end, and the list keeps 64px
  for it in every row, so the columns stay lined up.
- A cell is words, or a part: an **AvatarGroup** with its number in
  **MetaText**, 6px apart. A cell not read yet shows a dash.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.NotReadYet} />

- It takes the whole width it is given. The name and what it is about take
  the room on the left; the facts sit together on the right, before the end,
  24px apart, as are the name and the first fact (Katerina, 17 November:
  "everything is crammed together on the left … we need air for them to
  breathe"). From 16 November the name was capped at 384px with the facts
  beside it; they read as one crammed block.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.OnAWidePage} />

## When not

- The rows of a sidebar → **NavItem**, in a **Sidebar** or a **NavTree**.
- The list beside the thing that is open → **ListColumn**.
- A link alone on its line, in a file's text → **LinkRow**.
- A table someone writes, in a document → the editor's table.

## How

```tsx
import { Button, ListTable, ListTableRow } from '@estiva-app/ui'

<ListTable
  aria-label="Topics"
  columns={[
    { label: 'Team', width: 'large' },
    { label: 'Members', width: 'small', keep: 'low' },
    { label: 'Active', width: 'medium', keep: 'low' },
  ]}
  end
>
  {topics.map((topic) => (
    <ListTableRow
      key={topic.id}
      href={topic.href}
      label={topic.title}
      description={topic.summary}
      icon={<KindIcon kind="topic" />}
      cells={[topic.team, topic.members, topic.active]}
      end={topic.yours ? undefined : <Button size="small" variant="outlined" onClick={() => join(topic)}>Join</Button>}
    />
  ))}
</ListTable>
```

- **The row is the link**: a click anywhere on it opens its thing, and the
  keyboard ring goes round the whole row. What is in `end` stays its own
  control, above the link.
- It draws the headings, the row's look (8px corners, the fill under the
  pointer, no edge), the widths, the dashes and the giving way. Write none of
  them; give it words and parts.
- Its headings line up with their cells because both come from `columns`:
  never draw a heading row of your own over it.


### Cells a row has no fact for

A list of several kinds keeps one set of columns, so like facts line up down
the page. A cell is one of three things:

- **Words or a part**: the fact.
- **An empty string**: nothing. This row's kind has no such fact (a team has
  no progress).
- **Absent** (`undefined`): a dash. The fact is not read yet.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.FactsARowDoesNotHave} />

### Another app's row

`external` opens the row in a new tab, with `noopener noreferrer`, as `Link`
does: a file another app opens, listed here.

With no `href` a row leads nowhere: its name is plain words and it has no fill
under the pointer, so nothing looks like a link that is not (a file whose app
gave no address to open it at).

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.OpensInANewTab} />

### Rows under a row

Give a row `children`, more `ListTableRow`s, and they fold under it in the
same columns: a document's pages under the document. Shut until opened
(`defaultOpen`, or `open` with `onOpenChange` to own it). Under the pointer
the row's icon turns into the arrow, and only the arrow folds, as a
`NavItem`'s does; the row still opens its own thing. Its rows sit 24px in,
under the tree's line; only the name gives the room, so the facts keep their
columns.

<Canvas of={ListTableStories.RowsUnderARow} />

## What it owns

| It does | So you never write |
|---|---|
| Draws the column headings over the cells | a row of headings by hand |
| Hides columns by its own width, `low` first | `hidden sm:block` on a column |
| Draws a row's fill, its 8px corners and no edge | `rounded-md hover:bg-bg-hover` on a row |
| Makes the whole row the link, its end above it | a covering link and `relative` by hand |
| Gives the name the room on the left and puts the facts together on the right, 24px apart | a width of your own on the name, a gap by hand |
| Folds a row's rows under it, its icon turning into the arrow, in the same columns | a nested list, an arrow or an indent by hand |
| Opens another app's row in a new tab, safely | `target` and `rel` by hand |

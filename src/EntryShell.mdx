# EntryShell

What an app shows while nobody is signed in to it: its logo, one line saying
why, and at most one button.

<Canvas of={EntryShellStories.NoSession} />

It is the same canvas in every state, a logo and at most one affordance,
because the thing it must never look like is a separate sign-in page for an
identity the person already holds.

## When

- **`busy`**: entry is still being decided. The logo alone, pulsing, revealed
  after 150ms, so an entry that resolves at once flashes nothing.
- **A `message` and an `action`**: entry stopped and one thing would get the
  person in, such as continuing with a passkey or trying again.
- **A `message` alone**: entry stopped and nothing the person can do would
  help. No button, because a button that cannot work is worse than none.

<Canvas of={EntryShellStories.TokenRejected} />

## When not

- As a page inside the app's frame. It stands in *for* the whole app, around
  it, while entry is decided; inside the frame, nothing to show is an
  **EmptyState**.
- With anything beyond the logo. The one-line, one-button canvas is what the
  part exists to keep.

## How

```tsx
import { EntryShell } from '@estiva-app/ui'

<EntryShell logo={appLogo} busy />

<EntryShell
  logo={appLogo}
  message={copyForWhy}
  action={canRecover ? { label: continueLabel, onClick: startPasskey } : undefined}
/>
```

- **The words are the caller's.** A shared part has none of its own for why
  entry stopped; the apps pass the identity package's, so every app says the
  same thing.
- **`action.onClick` runs from the tap itself.** A passkey prompt needs the
  person's gesture, so start it there, not behind anything asynchronous.
- **The logo is drawn as given.** Hand it at the size the app draws its mark
  at; the shell does not resize it.

## What it owns

Nothing. It only draws. Clicks and keys are yours.
